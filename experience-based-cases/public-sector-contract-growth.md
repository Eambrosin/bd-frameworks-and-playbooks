# Experience-Based Case — Public-Sector Contract Growth & Multi-Site Execution

> **Experience-based, anonymized case.** This case reflects Eduardo Ambrosin's professional experience. Client/entity names and operationally sensitive details are intentionally omitted.
>
> **Role / period:** Executive Director, Port Service | **Feb 2017–Jul 2024**

## Context

A Brazilian services business operated in a competitive environment combining **public procurement, private contracts and multi-site service delivery**.

The commercial challenge was not limited to winning tenders. Growth required coordinating:

- opportunity identification
- bid preparation
- contract negotiation
- operational feasibility
- workforce mobilization
- compliance with client requirements
- multi-city execution after award

## Commercial Objective

Grow the active contract portfolio while maintaining the ability to mobilize and operate across multiple locations.

## Constraints

The environment required balancing several risks:

- formal procurement requirements
- price and margin discipline
- operational capacity after award
- recruitment and training at scale
- contract-compliance obligations
- different execution conditions across cities and clients

## Actions

The commercial and operational approach included:

1. screening public and private opportunities before committing bid resources;
2. coordinating tender documentation and commercial submissions;
3. evaluating execution requirements before pricing and commitment;
4. supporting negotiation and contract structuring;
5. planning workforce mobilization and training after award;
6. monitoring the active portfolio across multiple locations;
7. connecting commercial decisions with operational delivery rather than treating the bid as the end of the sales process.

## Result

Across the broader seven-year period:

- **USD 4M+** in total secured contract value;
- **USD 3M+** from public-sector contracts;
- **USD 1M+** from private-sector contracts;
- **1,000+ people** employed cumulatively across the operating period;
- activity across **15 cities**;
- the active contract portfolio expanded by approximately **40%**.

## What This Demonstrates

This case is relevant to Business Development because it required more than lead generation or proposal writing.

It combined:

- B2G opportunity qualification
- public procurement
- pricing and commercial judgment
- negotiation
- contract execution awareness
- operational scalability
- stakeholder coordination
- risk management

## Lessons

### 1. Winning the contract is not the final commercial objective

A bid that cannot be executed profitably or reliably is not a good commercial win.

### 2. Qualification matters before proposal effort

Tender preparation consumes time and resources. Opportunity selection should consider both award potential and post-award execution requirements.

### 3. Commercial and operational teams must share the same assumptions

Pricing, staffing, service levels and implementation conditions should be validated before commitment.

### 4. B2G growth rewards process discipline

Documentation, deadlines, compliance and repeatability can become a competitive capability.

## How I Would Structure It Today

Using the Commercial Intelligence approach developed in this portfolio, I would formalize the process as:

```text
OPPORTUNITY DISCOVERY
        ↓
ELIGIBILITY / REQUIREMENTS CHECK
        ↓
COMMERCIAL ATTRACTIVENESS
        ↓
EXECUTION FEASIBILITY
        ↓
BID / NO-BID DECISION
        ↓
PROPOSAL & NEGOTIATION
        ↓
MOBILIZATION
        ↓
CONTRACT PERFORMANCE REVIEW
        ↓
RENEWAL / EXPANSION
```

The main improvement would be to preserve evidence and explicit decision criteria earlier in the bid/no-bid process so commercial effort is concentrated on opportunities that are both attractive and executable.
